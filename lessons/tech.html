Always connected. Actually present?

B2 · Technology · 15, 30 or 50 minutes

Explore attention, digital habits, and the choices behind the screen.

Open teacher and presentation views →

Warm-up

Which phone feature saves you time, and which one takes more time than you intend?

Vocabulary

  • distraction — something that takes your attention away
  • intentional — done with a clear purpose
  • trade-off — a benefit you give up to gain something else
  • accessible — easy to reach or use

Read and discuss

A group of friends tried putting their phones in a basket during dinner. The conversation became longer, but one friend felt uneasy because she was waiting for an important call. They changed the rule: phones could stay nearby, with non-essential alerts switched off. Their experiment suggested that a useful boundary should support people rather than punish them.

  1. What did the friends try first?
  2. Why did one friend feel uneasy?
  3. How did they adapt the rule?

Speaking task

Design three realistic digital boundaries for a shared workspace. Discuss exceptions and explain why each boundary helps.

Useful language: While I can see the benefit… / A reasonable compromise might be… / It depends on whether…

Reading answer key
  1. Putting phones in a basket during dinner.
  2. She was waiting for an important call.
  3. They kept phones nearby and switched off non-essential alerts.
